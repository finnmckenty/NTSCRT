## librashader (the CRT shader runtime)

- **What:** `librashader.dylib`, built unmodified from SnowflakePowered/librashader at commit `76462c03`, and its C API headers (`Sources/CrtAppBridge/include/librashader.h`, `librashader_ld.h`).
- **License:** the runtime is MPL-2.0 (librashader offers MPL-2.0 OR GPL-3.0-only; NTSCRT distributes it under MPL-2.0, text in `licenses/MPL-2.0.txt`). MPL-2.0 applies file by file, so it covers librashader's files only. The headers are MIT, Copyright 2022 chyyran, with the notice kept in the files.
- **Source:** the Source Code Form of the bundled librashader is at https://github.com/SnowflakePowered/librashader/tree/76462c03 ; DEVELOPMENT.md has the exact build commands.

## Rust crates inside the two libraries

Both libraries statically include open-source Rust crates (214 in total, including glslang and SPIRV-Cross through their `-sys` crates). All are under permissive licenses (MIT, Apache-2.0, BSD, ISC, Zlib, CC0, Unlicense), except for librashader's own crates and two small MPL-2.0 crates (`smartstring`, `persy`). Every crate, its license, and its license and copyright text is listed in [`licenses/rust-crates.txt`](licenses/rust-crates.txt), which is regenerated by `scripts/gen-rust-licenses.py`.

## RetroArch CRT shaders (slang-shaders)

- **What:** the `crt/` and `include/` folders of libretro's slang-shaders, copied into the app as shader source files and compiled at runtime by librashader. They are separate works, not part of NTSCRT's code.
- **License:** each shader carries its own license in its files; slang-shaders has no single license for the collection. Among the ones the CRT menu offers: crt-royale is GPL-2.0-or-later (its `LICENSE.TXT` ships with it), crt-aperture and crt-easymode are GPL, crt-hyllian is MIT, and crtsim is public domain. GPL texts: `licenses/GPL-2.0.txt`, `licenses/GPL-3.0.txt`. The shaders ship as source, unmodified, with their notices intact.
- **Source:** https://github.com/libretro/slang-shaders

## Credits without bundled code

- The Glitch stage's design follows Trevor Blackwell's analogtv in xscreensaver; NTSCRT's receiver simulation is its own code, written from that design.
- The Digital video stage's quantization tables are the reference tables from the JPEG standard (ITU-T T.81, Annex K).
